Warm-up 1

Acceleration and distance on a frictionless surface

An object of mass m starts from rest and is pulled to the right along a horizontal frictionless surface by a constant tension T. Derive expressions for its acceleration a and the distance D moved after time t. State assumptions.

Answer

a = T/m     and     D = Tt²/(2m)

Assumptions include constant mass, constant T, negligible friction/drag, horizontal motion, and the object starts from rest.

Workings

AP equation-sheet start pointF⃗net = m a⃗
  1. Take right as positive. The only unbalanced horizontal force is T, so T = ma.
  2. Therefore a = T/m.
  3. For the distance, start from the AP kinematics equation x = x0 + vx0t + ½axt².
  4. Since the object starts from rest, vx0 = 0, and with D = x - x0: D = ½at².
  5. Substitute a = T/m: D = Tt²/(2m).

Assumptions: friction/drag negligible; T is constant; mass is constant; the surface is horizontal so vertical forces balance; the motion begins from rest.

Warm-up 2

Applied force with a resistive force

An object of mass m accelerates to the right with acceleration a. A resistive force R acts to the left. Derive the applied force F.

Worksheet notation note: the written question calls the applied force F, while the diagram labels that rightward force T. The solution uses F, matching the written question.

Answer

F = ma + R

Workings

AP equation-sheet start pointF⃗net = m a⃗
  1. Take right as positive.
  2. The net horizontal force is F - R.
  3. Apply Newton's second law: F - R = ma.
  4. Rearrange: F = ma + R.

Warm-up 3

Two connected bodies - derive the string tension

Two bodies of masses m1 and m2 are connected by a light, inextensible string. A force P pulls the right-hand body to the right. Derive the tension T.

Worksheet correction: the sentence says P is applied to B1, but the diagram clearly shows P applied to the right-hand body m2. It also says “tension and F” where the applied force is P. The solution follows the diagram, which is the physically consistent interpretation.

Answer

T = m1P / (m1 + m2)

Workings

AP equation-sheet start pointa⃗sys = ΣF⃗ / msys   and   F⃗net = m a⃗
  1. Because the string is light, taut, and inextensible, both blocks have the same acceleration magnitude a.
  2. Treat both blocks as one system. The external horizontal force is P and the system mass is m1 + m2, so a = P/(m1 + m2).
  3. Now isolate the left block m1. Its only horizontal force is the tension, so T = m1a.
  4. Substitute the system acceleration: T = m1P/(m1 + m2).

Suggested markscheme - 5 points

EvidencePoints

Uses a system form of Newton's second law.1
Obtains a = P/(m1+m2).1
Applies T = m1a to the left block.1
Obtains T = m1P/(m1+m2).1
Warm-up 4

Three identical blocks - derive T1 and T2

Three identical blocks, each of mass m, are connected by two light strings and pulled to the right by an external force F. Derive the tensions T1 and T2.

Answer

T1 = F/3     and     T2 = 2F/3

Workings

AP equation-sheet start pointa⃗sys = ΣF⃗ / msys   and   F⃗net = m a⃗
  1. All three blocks share the same acceleration because the connecting strings are taut and inextensible.
  2. For the three-block system: a = F/(3m).
  3. For the left block alone, T1 = ma = m[F/(3m)] = F/3.
  4. For the left two blocks as a subsystem, the only external horizontal force on that subsystem is T2: T2 = (2m)a = 2m[F/(3m)] = 2F/3.
T1 = F/3,   T2 = 2F/3

Suggested markscheme - 5 points

EvidencePoints

Correctly treats the left two blocks as a subsystem (or uses the middle block equation).1

AP Physics 1 Practice FRQ - QQT

Three blocks and two pulleys

Blocks B1, B2, and B3 have masses m1, m2, and m3, with m1 < m2 < m3. The strings and pulleys are ideal and resistance is negligible. Rightward motion of B2 corresponds to B1 moving up and B3 moving down.

Katty proposes: a = (m3 - m1)g / m2,   T1 = m1g,   T2 = m3g.
FRQ Part A(i)

Consistency with constant rightward acceleration

Without deciding whether Katty's equation is physically correct, is experimental result (i) - constant acceleration to the right - consistent with her expression for a?

Answer

Yes. Katty's expression predicts a constant positive value because the masses and g are constant and m3 > m1.

Reasoning

  1. Katty's expression is a = (m3-m1)g/m2.
  2. During a trial, all three masses and g are constant, so the expression gives a constant acceleration.
  3. Since m3 > m1, the numerator is positive, so a>0 when right is positive.
  4. Therefore result (i) is consistent with the proposed expression.

Suggested markscheme - 2 points

EvidencePoints
States that the result is consistent with the expression.1
Justifies that the expression gives a constant positive acceleration because masses and g are constant and m3>m1.1
FRQ Part A(ii)

Doubling all three masses

Is experimental result (ii) - acceleration unchanged when all three masses are doubled - consistent with Katty's expression for a?

Answer

Yes. Doubling every mass multiplies both numerator and denominator by 2, so the factor cancels and a is unchanged.

Reasoning

Substitute 2m1, 2m2, 2m3 into Katty's expression:

a' = [(2m3 - 2m1)g]/(2m2) = [2(m3-m1)g]/(2m2) = a

Therefore the experimental result is consistent with her expression.

Suggested markscheme - 2 points

EvidencePoints
States that the result is consistent with the expression.1
Shows or explains that the common factor of 2 cancels from numerator and denominator.1
FRQ Part B

Identify the invalid physical assumption

Katty is told her equations are not correct. Identify one invalid physical assumption implied by her equations and explain why it is invalid.

Answer

Her tension equations T1=m1g and T2=m3g imply that the hanging blocks have zero net force and therefore zero acceleration. That is impossible while the taut strings make them accelerate with the same magnitude as B2.

Reasoning

Useful physics relationF⃗net = m a⃗
  1. For B1, Katty sets T1=m1g, which would make the vertical net force zero.
  2. For B3, she sets T2=m3g, which would also make the vertical net force zero.
  3. Zero net force would mean zero acceleration for B1 and B3.
  4. But with taut, inextensible strings, B1 and B3 must have the same nonzero acceleration magnitude as B2. Hence the assumption is invalid.

Suggested markscheme - 2 points

EvidencePoints
Identifies that T=mg assumes zero net force / equilibrium for a hanging block.1
Explains that the taut strings require the hanging blocks to accelerate with B2, so their net forces cannot be zero.1
FRQ Part C

Derive the correct acceleration of B2

Derive a correct expression for the acceleration a of B2 in terms of m1, m2, m3, and g. Begin with a fundamental physics principle or an equation from the reference information.

Answer

a = (m3 - m1)g / (m1 + m2 + m3)

Workings

AP equation-sheet start pointF⃗net = m a⃗

Use a positive direction along each block's motion: B1 upward, B2 rightward, B3 downward. The taut strings give all three blocks the same acceleration magnitude a.

  1. For B1: T1 - m1g = m1a.
  2. For B2: T2 - T1 = m2a.
  3. For B3: m3g - T2 = m3a.
  4. Add the three equations. The internal tensions cancel: (m3-m1)g = (m1+m2+m3)a.
  5. Therefore a = (m3-m1)g/(m1+m2+m3).

Suggested markscheme - 5 points

Correct equation for B2: T2-T1=m2a.1
Correct equation for B3: m3g-T2=m3a.1
